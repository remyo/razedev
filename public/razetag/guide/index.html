01 / GET STARTED

Start with one item.

You don’t need every detail right away. Save the essentials, then add more as you go.

Add → Create details manually

  1. Start with Essentials

    Give the item a name, category, and condition. Use View more when your category isn’t in the quick list.

  2. Add quantity and buying cost

    Pick the currency, enter how many pieces you have, and enter the purchase price per piece. When using another currency, review the conversion choice before saving.

  3. Set a target, then save

    Add an optional selling price and photos. Open the extra sections only when you need more detail, then review and save.

FOR EXAMPLE

8 controllers × ₱600 each
One inventory listing. Eight pieces. ₱4,800 in purchase cost.

What else can I add?

Open Category-specific details, Dates, More details, or Additional costs / deductions for the information you need. These cover buying dates, a barcode, storage location, package contents, notes, and costs such as inbound shipping or repairs.

Add photos from the camera or gallery on your phone, or import image files on your Mac. When editing, Save becomes available after you change something. You can return later to finish the details.

02 / LESS TYPING

Let the camera help.

Use a photo for the listing, or a reader for a head start on the details. Always review the result before saving.

01

Take a regular photo

Open Add → Take a photo. Regular is the default camera mode. You can also add pictures directly inside an item.

Use a clear background and fill the frame without cutting off the product.

02

Read a label or barcode

Scan a barcode to look for a saved match. Read label helps you capture text from the product name area instead of typing it all.

A barcode isn’t a guaranteed worldwide product lookup. Check the suggested details.

03

Read a collectible card

Choose Card mode, hold one card upright inside the frame, and keep the name and card number readable. Review the suggestions.

Enable Card or Console camera modes in Settings → Camera if you want them available.

POKÉMON CARD LOOKUP

Find a reference. Confirm the match.

For Card items, use Search beside the name to enter the set and card number, or browse packs and cards. Check the language and edition before choosing Use card.

A reference is not a condition photo

Keep your own front and back photos to show wear. Catalog artwork can help identify the card, but doesn’t replace pictures of your actual item.

Prices are a guide—not a promise

When available, market references show their source, currency, and update time. They don’t automatically change your saved purchase or selling price.

Reading tips & photo tools

Avoid reflections, steady your phone, and move back slightly if the camera can’t focus. You can edit any suggestion manually. In an item’s photo preview, look for the available crop, background-removal, and save-copy actions. Review the finished image before using it.

Card reading and catalog matching are different: camera text suggestions can be wrong, and a catalog match still needs your confirmation. Online catalog searches, artwork downloads, and fresh price or exchange references need internet access when they aren’t already cached.

03 / YOUR STOCK, SORTED

Find the right item in seconds.

Browse by category, search for a name or code, and switch between a photo grid and a compact list.

Inventory → search, category, or filter

  1. Narrow down the list

    Use the status chips for All, Sold, or available stock. Filters can narrow the results by category, location, minimum cost in a chosen currency, purchase dates, and age.

  2. Open an item for its story

    See its photos, quantity, costs, notes, and related bundle or transaction. Edit the listing or record a sale when it’s ready.

  3. Use multi-select for a group

    Tap the selection control, then tick the items you need. Review the selected count before choosing an action.

Older stock needs attention?

Tap an age group on Home to see just those items. It’s a quick way to spot stock you may want to photograph again, reprice, or sell together.

Archive or delete—which one?

Archive hides an item from your active stock without permanently deleting it. Open Archive to review, restore, or select items for permanent deletion.

Permanent deletion requires confirmation. Make a backup first if you might need the item or its history again.

STOCK HISTORY

Correct a count. Keep the reason.

Open an item → Stock history → Adjust stock. Choose Count correction for a miscount, or Damaged or Lost for pieces you need to remove. Enter the quantity and explain what happened.

Damaged or lost pieces keep their purchase costs in archived records; removing them does not create a sale.

Bought more? Use Restock.

Enter the new quantity, unit cost, and purchase date. Restock creates a separate purchase lot so the new purchase does not rewrite earlier costs or sales.

Follow the changes

Stock history shows the count before and after a change, its reason, and related lots. Use See all for older entries. Earlier movements are not reconstructed.

A NEW PURCHASE, NOT A MISCOUNT

You have 5 controllers bought at ₱600 each, then buy 3 more at ₱650 each. Use Restock for the three new pieces. Keep both purchase lots and their costs clear.

Why can’t I adjust this item?

Available actions depend on the item’s history. A pending delivery or grading submission can block stock changes; finish that workflow first. Count correction is not offered for purchase-bundle children or returned lots. Use only the actions shown, rather than changing a past sale to correct today’s stock.

04 / ONE PURCHASE, MANY POSSIBILITIES

Bought together.
Sell together—or separately.

A purchase bundle keeps the original deal connected, even when you sell its pieces at different times.

Add → Purchase a bundle

  1. Record what you paid

    Name the bundle, choose the currency, and enter the total purchase amount.

  2. Add the items inside

    Add at least two item lines. Each child item has its own name, category, quantity, photos, and resale target. Shared values help when several new lines use the same settings.

  3. Choose how to share the cost

    Use Equal, Resale value, or Manual. Review the allocated costs before saving.

A WEEKEND PICKUP

A console, a controller, and a game bought for ₱12,000. Keep the purchase together, then sell each part when a buyer is ready.

Equal

Split the total evenly between item lines. A line with several pieces is still one line.

Resale value

Give more of the cost to higher-value items, using resale price × quantity as the weighting. Add resale estimates first; without them, the split can behave like Equal.

Manual

Enter the cost allocation yourself. The item totals must match the bundle’s total.

After saving the bundle

Before selling any children, use Edit bundle to update the purchase or manage its contents. After a child sale, the original cost, allocation, and contents are locked to protect the sale history; purchase notes and other allowed details can still be edited. Children can be found from Inventory search.

Quantity means pieces, not duplicate listings. Sell part of one child’s quantity with its Sell action. Sell selected or Sell all remaining combines whole available item lines. Deleting a child asks for confirmation and can take effect immediately—read the confirmation carefully.

05 / RECORD THE RESULT

Sold three? Keep five in stock.

Record only what actually sold. RazeTag keeps the remaining pieces available and connects the sale to your history.

Open an available item → Mark as sold → Paid now

  1. Enter the quantity sold

    If you have more than one piece, enter the number the buyer purchased.

  2. Enter the total sale amount

    This is the amount for all selected pieces, not a price per piece. Open More sale details for the date, channel, fees, packing, or shipping. Don’t add the same expense twice.

  3. Review and confirm

    Check the sale summary before saving. Open Home → Sales to review sold items, deductions, and profit.

Selling-channel presets can fill common fee assumptions. Check them for this sale; a preset is a starting point, not a guarantee of the platform’s final charges.

06 / WHEN A SALE CHANGES

Keep the refund story clear.

Money coming back to the buyer and stock coming back to you are two different things.

Open the sold item’s management page → Refund

  1. Enter the money returned

    The form starts from the sold amount. Change it to the amount you actually refund.

  2. Check whether the item came back

    A money-only refund doesn’t put stock back into Inventory. Use the physical-return option only when it is offered and you have the item back.

  3. Review the history

    Open Return & refund history to see the refund record and related transaction. Inspect returned stock before making it available again.

Returned does not mean ready to sell.

Eligible returned stock is marked Returned for inspection. Check its condition, then relist it when ready. The physical-return option applies to the full eligible sold lot, not a custom subset of it.

Why don’t I see the return option?

Sales of purchase-bundle children and multi-item bundle sales allow money-only refunds here, not physical restocking. The return option also won’t reopen an already-refunded record. Use only the options shown for that sale.

07 / FOLLOW THROUGH

Track the handoff.
Don’t lose track of payment.

Keep the delivery steps connected to the item, without confusing a parcel’s status with money received.

Home → Sales → Deliveries & payments

01

Reserve the items

Open an available item → Mark as sold → Deposit / pay later. Enter the quantity and agreed total price, plus any courier, tracking, follow-up date, and notes. The reserved pieces stay out of available stock.

02

Record each payment

Open the delivery → Record payment. Enter only the money already received, then choose its method, date, and optional notes. Check Received, Balance due, and Payment history.

03

Clear the balance

Record the final payment, or choose Receive remaining balance when it has actually arrived. Receipts appear in Cash flow as they arrive; the completed sale and profit are recorded only when fully paid.

A DEPOSIT TODAY, THE REST LATER

For a ₱12,500 order, record ₱5,000 received. Balance due is ₱7,500. Record that remainder when it arrives—don’t enter the whole ₱12,500 a second time.

Delivered and paid are still separate.

Use Mark as shipped and Mark as delivered as the handoff progresses. These are manual updates, not live courier tracking. Marking a parcel delivered does not clear its payment balance. Home’s To do list helps you find follow-ups.

Cancelling a part-paid delivery

Cancel only once the reserved items are with you. If you received money, return it to the buyer first and confirm that in the cancellation form. RazeTag records the refund; it does not move money for you.

08 / THE BIGGER PICTURE

Three views. Three useful questions.

Sales, Monthly budget, and Cash flow help you understand different parts of the same business.

SALES

Did I make a profit?

Review completed sales, deductions, and profit for the period you choose.

Home → Sales

MONTHLY BUDGET

How much can I buy?

Set an optional spending limit and capital. Track the month’s purchases against the allowance.

Tools → Monthly budget

CASH FLOW

Where did the money go?

Browse monthly money in and out, then open a month and its transaction records.

Tools → Cash flow

Your first monthly budget

  1. Pick the month

    Open Monthly budget and select the month you want to review.

  2. Set a limit if you want one

    Tap the spending-limit area to enter a limit. Leave it unset for flexible tracking; you can reset it later.

  3. Add capital, then watch progress

    Set a monthly or global capital amount if useful. The page shows purchases, sales, profit, and how the month is progressing.

A spending limit is not your bank balance.

Budget remaining is your limit minus purchases. Capital remaining is starting capital minus purchases—sales don’t refill it automatically. Cash flow’s estimated balance includes recorded money in and out; it is not connected to your bank account.

SAVE A FIXED CONVERSION

Buy in one currency. Keep a clear cost.

Choose your usual currency in Settings → Currency & market. When adding an eligible item in another currency, review Convert to your chosen currency when saving. You can turn it off to keep the original currency.

The item’s prices and matching-currency deductions are converted once and rounded to whole currency units. Those saved values are then used throughout the app; they do not change with tomorrow’s rates.

Keep the original story

Open the item → Pricing → information button to see the original amounts, saved conversion, rate, and date. If only an older saved rate is available, review it before agreeing to use it.

Existing history stays intact

Changing your display currency does not convert all old records. An eligible active item can be explicitly converted when editing; name or photo changes alone do not reprice it. Existing sales, refunds, returned lots, archived stock, and purchase-bundle allocations are not automatically converted.

See a month’s transaction list

Open a month in Cash flow, then tap a transaction. The record explains the money movement and links to the related item or sale. Part-payments appear on their receipt dates, even while the order still has a balance.

A saved conversion or just an estimate?

Settings → Currency & market → Automatic conversion shows optional converted price estimates without changing saved records. Tools → Currency calculator is a separate quick comparison. Neither is the same as choosing a fixed conversion when saving an item.

Check the rate date. Reference rates are not your bank’s actual exchange charge. If a total warns that amounts in other currencies are excluded or unavailable, do not read those missing amounts as zero.

Less busywork.
More time for your next find.

Start with Listing tools to prepare stock for sale. Other tools help you track work, spending, and grading.

Tools → Listing tools

  1. Copy a listing

    Search or choose a category, then tap the item’s copy button. Review the public description and selling price. Choose Copy text, Share photos + text, or Save photos; these prepare a post but never publish it for you.

  2. Create a printable QR label

    Tap the QR button beside an item. Choose Print style, then Save label to export the label image. Scan a shelf label with the scanner button in Listing tools on a phone.

  3. Plan a combined sale

    Choose Bundle planner (shown as Bundle on a narrow screen), select at least two eligible listings in one currency, then tap Plan bundle. Review the proposed total and deductions before Continue to bundle sale. Cancel leaves stock unchanged.

✓

Tasks

Review the app’s suggested follow-ups, then open an item to resolve what needs attention. This is a work queue based on your stock—not a blank to-do notebook.

↗

Private details stay out of listing text

Purchase costs, seller details, storage location, and private notes are excluded from the public description. Review the generated text and your photos before sharing; photos can reveal private content, and existing location metadata is not removed.

▦

QR print styles

Choose Classic, Rounded, or Dots patterns; Black, Forest, or Indigo ink; Item label or QR only; and an optional Centre logo. Switch off the logo for very small labels. Print on white, keep the clear margin, and test one physical label before a batch.

◇

Your plan is not a sale

Bundle selections and planner scenarios are temporary. Stock changes only after you continue to the sale flow, review it, and confirm. Items that cannot join the proposed bundle remain unavailable for selection.

≋

Buying checklists

Run through category-specific checks before buying a card, console, game, or other supported item. Use it as a reminder, not a guarantee of condition.

☆

Card grading

Track a submission, fees, progress, and returned cards. Keep the costs and outcome connected to the cards you own.

A QR label is a local record link, not a public product page.

The code contains only the item’s RazeTag ID—not prices, location, notes, seller details, barcode, or serial number. Another phone needs the matching active inventory record. Local sharing can transfer it. The printed Item label still shows the product name, category, and condition.

Save label saves a PNG to Photos on a phone or offers Save As on Mac. On iOS, label saving asks only to add photos; Android uses the RazeTag Labels album. Print-style choices stay on this device.

Walk through card grading

Open an available Card item → Send for grading. Enter the company, sent date, and fees you have already paid. One card is separated from a quantity lot; the remaining pieces stay in stock.

Track it from Tools → Card grading or Home. Update its progress manually. When the card is physically back, choose Card returned, add its grade or certificate if available, and confirm. Paid fees are already counted as an item expense—don’t enter them twice.

More tools, shown in the app
What is saved, and what is just a quick check?

Inventory records and grading submissions are saved. Treat the bundle planner and checklist ticks as temporary working tools rather than saved purchases or permanent inspection reports.

10 / THE CUSTOMER VIEW

Show the product.
Keep the buying details private.

Showcase is a read-only product browser on your device. Choose the stock a customer can see, then hand over the phone with confidence about what is displayed.

Tools → Showcase

  1. Choose what goes on display

    Tap Add items in an empty Showcase, or open the settings cog → Manage showcase items. Search and select your products, then Save. Select all matches uses the current search; Clear selection clears the whole draft. Removing an item from Showcase does not delete it from Inventory.

  2. Browse in grids or cards

    Search by name or card set, choose a category, and switch between the grid and cards buttons. Swipe through cards or use the arrows. Tap a product for its larger public preview; tap the preview or backdrop again to close it. Back, or Escape on Mac, also closes the preview.

  3. Set the customer view

    Open the settings cog to change Show selling prices, Show product details, Show mascot, and Mascot style. Review your photos and any custom fields marked for public listings before showing them.

  4. Lock before handing over

    Settings cog → Lock showcase → Lock. Authenticate with your device’s PIN, passcode, fingerprint, or Face ID. The lock keeps navigation to private app pages out of Showcase and survives closing and reopening the app. Tap the padlock and authenticate again to unlock.

Selected stock only

Only the eligible products you chose appear. Sold, archived, and out-for-grading items stay hidden. Newly added inventory does not join Showcase automatically.

Public listing fields

Purchase costs, seller details, storage location, private notes, serial numbers, and grading certificate numbers stay out of the view. Photos and public custom fields still need your review.

No publishing or sales

Showcase does not publish a website, send inventory to a customer’s device, or record a sale. Unlock and use the normal sale flow when the buyer is ready.

Showcase lock is an app view lock, not a phone lock.

It needs working device authentication. It does not prevent screenshots, leaving the app, or access to other phone apps. Use your phone’s separate app-pinning or Guided Access controls if you need that restriction.

12 / ONE INVENTORY, TWO DEVICES

Keep working.
Keep each other up to date.

Sync is a two-way session for your full inventory, including private details. Use it for your own trusted devices—not to give a teammate restricted selling access. Choose Shop team for that.

On both devices: Settings → Local network → Sync devices

  1. Read the notice on both devices

    Connect to the same Wi-Fi, update both apps, and accept the full-inventory notice. Use devices you trust with your private purchase and sales information.

  2. Start and approve

    Choose Start sync on the host. Join from the other device using the displayed QR or nearby-device code, then approve the connection on the host. On a Mac, use the code option.

  3. Work with the apps open

    You can leave the sync page and work on inventory. New records and edits made on only one device sync automatically. The host connects with one device at a time.

Sync includes private financial details.

Unlike selected sharing, sync brings across the full inventory: available, sold, and archived records, photos, purchase and sale history, expenses, deliveries, and grading records. Make a backup on both devices before the first session.

Review competing edits

If both devices change the same record, review it before choosing which version to keep. Deletions also need review; quantities are not added together to settle a conflict.

With a Mac, the host decides

In a session involving a Mac, the device that chose Start sync reviews conflicts. Accept incoming or Keep local decides the version on both devices. The host can be your phone or Mac.

Your preferences stay local

Theme, app protection, budgets, and device preferences are not copied. Sync keeps inventory aligned; it does not make the two devices’ settings identical. For limited selling access without a full private copy, use Shop team instead.

Stopping, reconnecting, and nearby reminders

Keep both apps open and unlocked on the same Wi-Fi. Backgrounding or locking a phone ends the session. A Mac can keep syncing while its window stays visible, even when another app has focus; hiding or minimizing it, locking the screen, or sleep stops sync. Pair and approve again to restart.

After an approved session, Sync device nearby can offer to open sync when your remembered device is nearby. It does not approve a connection or transfer inventory by itself. Choose Not now to dismiss it.

If something needs attention

Read the message before accepting another version. If a related group cannot be applied safely, it stays marked Needs attention while other changes can continue. Demo inventory syncs only with demo inventory, and personal inventory only with personal inventory.

Check that both devices use the same Wi-Fi and allow Local Network access. Guest networks may block device-to-device connections. Sync is a foreground session, not a background service or a replacement for backups.

13 / SELLING WITH A TEAM

One owner.
The right access for each teammate.

Shop team gives another device a limited selling catalog. It is different from full-inventory sync: the owner’s app checks permissions and stock before confirming each change.

On both devices: Settings → Local network → Shop team

  1. Back up and choose the owner

    Back up both devices first. If you already have unrestricted shared copies, reconcile them with ordinary Sync devices before changing workflows. On the owner device, choose Set up shop, enter a Shop name, check Use this phone as the shop owner, then Create shop. Its current inventory becomes the shop’s master record.

  2. Invite the member device

    Owner: Invite / reconnect. Member: Join shop. Keep both updated apps open and unlocked on the same Wi-Fi, then pair with the displayed QR or nearby-device code.

  3. Agree on permissions

    The owner chooses Choose role, selects Manager or Staff, adjusts permissions and the offline policy, then Save role. The member reads the role and policy and taps Accept role. The catalog is not shared before acceptance.

  4. Record work while connected

    From a catalog product, choose Sell, enter Quantity sold and Total selling amount, then Confirm sale. This is the total for the sold quantity, not a per-piece price. Only the actions the owner allowed are available. Refresh to reload the catalog.

ROLE STARTING POINTS — THE OWNER CAN CUSTOMIZE MEMBER PERMISSIONS
RoleWhat it means
OwnerKeeps the master inventory, grants access, and handles full product details, photos, backups, physical returns, and permanent deletion. Ownership cannot be granted by invitation.
ManagerStarts with all supported member permissions. If Remove staff from this shop is allowed, can remove Staff—not the Owner, themselves, or another Manager.
StaffStarts with Record sales only. The owner can enable other supported actions individually; Staff cannot manage memberships.
Which actions can the owner allow?

Record sales; Add products; Edit product names; Change selling prices; Choose a different sale amount; Record money refunds; View purchase costs; Copy the selling catalog; and, for Managers only, Remove staff from this shop.

Add product creates a basic product; the owner adds photos, costs, and full details. Copy catalog copies names, quantities, and selling prices—not purchase costs or a restorable backup. A money refund uses Total refunded, the cumulative refund for that sale, not a new amount to add. It does not return stock.

A separate catalog

The member copy stays separate from personal inventory and currently has no product photos. Purchase costs are not sent unless View purchase costs is allowed.

A foreground connection

One owner session connects with one member at a time. Re-pair after stopping, locking, backgrounding, or session expiry. This is not cloud sync or an always-on connection.

An agreed offline policy

The owner sets Lock after days without sync and optional removal of an expired downloaded catalog. Removal affects the downloaded catalog, not personal inventory or the owner’s records.

Interrupted sale? Check it before recording again.

Reconnect and use Check / retry request for the pending action. If it cannot be verified after a restore or lost access, use Review with owner and compare the owner’s stock and sales history before choosing Already recorded or Not recorded. Do not create a second sale to guess the outcome.

Leaving, revoking, or ending the shop

The owner can change permissions or remove a member. Leave shop clears the member’s downloaded copy; leaving offline still needs the owner to revoke that device separately. Dissolve shop ends team access but preserves the owner’s inventory, photos, and financial history.

A disconnected device keeps its last accepted offline policy until authenticated contact. Roles cannot recall unrestricted copies previously received through sync or backups, or exported files. This is not a remote-wipe guarantee. An owner backup restore requires fresh member approval.

16 / A SMALL HABIT THAT MATTERS

Keep a copy.
Keep control.

Your inventory lives on your device. Sync helps you work across devices; a separate backup gives you a recovery point if something goes wrong.

01

Make a full backup

Open Settings → Backup & reports and export a RazeTag backup. Save the file somewhere you can reach even if you lose the phone.

Enable Settings → App protection → Encrypt new backups if you want password protection. Keep the password safe—it cannot be recovered if forgotten.

02

Restore with care

Restoring a full backup replaces your current personal inventory—it does not simply add a few items.

Make a fresh backup first. Use Local network’s review flow for selected incoming items.

03

Reports are not backups

Excel and CSV reports are readable, unencrypted exports and can contain private financial details. They cannot restore the app or its photos.

For a public sales post, use Tools → Listing tools → the item’s copy button instead.

Export a spreadsheet report

Settings → Backup & reports → Spreadsheet reports

  1. Choose a file format

    Select Excel (.xlsx) for status-colored rows, column filters, and a fixed header row, or CSV (.csv) for plain data.

  2. Choose the records

    Tap Inventory Excel or Sales Excel (the button names change to CSV for that format). Choose where to save the file and wait for the confirmation.

  3. Store the export securely

    Reports always use your personal inventory, even while Demo mode is open. Check the file before sending it to anyone; use a full RazeTag backup for recovery.

PRIVACY & CLEANUP

Private by choice.

Settings → App protection lets you protect sensitive actions and purchase details. Hide Home’s money totals when you don’t want them visible.

Free space without guessing

Settings → Storage & cleanup shows usage and cleanup choices. Clearing temporary cache is different from deleting saved listings or their photos.

Delete only what you mean to delete

Review sold-item date filters and the selected count before confirming. Back up first if you might need the transaction history again.

Permissions, in plain language

Camera

For taking item photos and using the barcode, QR, or label readers.

Photos / gallery

For importing pictures or saving exports. The permission depends on the action: on iOS, QR label saving uses add-only access; exporting listing photos to an album can require broader Photos access. Denial does not remove saved inventory.

Local network

For discovery, selected sharing, sync, and Shop team on the same Wi-Fi. Pair and approve the devices; being nearby does not authorize a connection. Guest networks may prevent devices from reaching each other.

Notifications

For the phone reminders you enable. Denying it does not erase your inventory.

Device authentication

For actions protected by App protection and for unlocking a locked Showcase. Your phone’s PIN, passcode, fingerprint, or Face ID is handled by the device.

Do I have to allow every permission?

No. Allow access for the feature you want to use. If you decline a permission, you can still enter item details manually. You can revisit permissions in your phone’s settings.
